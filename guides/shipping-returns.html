Shipping and Returns: What New Sellers Must Decide

A research-based guide to shipping and returns: what new sellers must decide. Use this checklist to compare real options and verify details before making a financial decision.

Fulfillment responsibilities

Clarify who packages orders, books carriers, manages customs and handles lost shipments. Supplier fulfillment does not eliminate seller obligations.

Delivery expectations

Distinguish handling time from transit time. Verify regions served and account for holidays and remote destinations.

Returns and refunds

Document damaged goods, cancellations, return windows, return shipping costs and refund timing consistent with applicable law.

International orders

Check customs duties, product restrictions and consumer protections in each destination.

Failure scenarios

Prepare responses to wrong addresses, chargebacks, supplier stockouts and missed delivery estimates.

Action checklist

  1. Write down your actual requirements and constraints.
  2. Verify current specifications, terms and costs with the provider.
  3. Compare at least two realistic alternatives.
  4. Document any unresolved questions before committing money.

Practical examples and checks

A hypothetical failure scenario

A customer orders an item for delivery within one country, but the supplier ships from another. The package is delayed by customs. If the store promised a guaranteed short delivery window without checking the origin, the customer receives misleading expectations. State handling and transit estimates separately.

Questions for a supplier

Where is inventory held? Who pays return postage? How are damaged goods documented? What tracking events are available? How quickly will support respond? Ask for written answers before listing the product.

Policy review checklist

Match the published policy to actual operations and applicable consumer law. Make cancellation instructions understandable, define refund timing, and provide a real contact method. Do not copy another retailer's policy without checking its suitability.

Evidence and decision quality

A useful policy also explains how a customer starts a return, which contact channel to use, what information is needed, and whether the item must be unused or in original packaging where legally permitted. Keep internal procedures aligned with the public policy. Review the policy whenever a fulfillment partner, destination country or shipping carrier changes.
